See how your measures relate, in Tableau

Correlation Matrix is a free viz extension that draws every pair of measures as a scatter, each measure's distribution on the diagonal, and how strongly they move together, explained in words.

Quick start

Three steps, about a minute.

  1. Download

    correlation.trex, a small manifest file that tells Tableau where the extension lives.

  2. Add it in Tableau

    Show Me → Viz Extensions → Access Local Viz Extensions, pick the file, then follow the prompts.

  3. Drag fields

    Two to eight measures to Measures (as AVG) and a row ID to Detail (one point per record). Optionally a dimension to Colour.

Then fine-tune it with Format Extension, below the tiles on the Marks card. On Tableau Cloud or Server, an admin first adds https://okhammahko.github.io to the site's extension allow list. This is a preview: tell us what works and what doesn't in the issue tracker.

Read it right

Click a number, or the ⤢ on a scatter, to look closer at one pair. Each pair is explained in words, with the caveats that apply.

Also included

  • Pearson r or Spearman ρ (rank-based: robust to outliers and curves); hover any number for both, n and the line's formula.
  • Plain-language readings with caveats when they apply: a curved or outlier-driven relationship, colour groups that disagree, few records.
  • Brushing: drag a rectangle in a scatter, or click a histogram bar, to select those records everywhere.
  • Outliers cut from the axes automatically (correlations still use every value), with a badge to show them all.
  • Median, mean and P5/P95 markers in the field's own number format; native Tableau tooltips, selection and actions.
  • Your data stays in Tableau: the extension draws inside the worksheet and sends nothing anywhere.
